# Screamer 2's menus: the original's screens, what they are made of, what the core can do

Study and plan (2026-11-04); built so far: the menu (`firmware/s2menu.c`, docs/development.md "The menu"), rows 1 to 4 of section 7, the 2D
toolkit with the LOADING screen and the pause menu, the save, the arcade pages, the end of the race with the best times, the GAME SETTINGS and HI SCORES pages
(section 6), from row 5 (2026-11-05) the 3D-o pages TEAM SELECTION, ENTER YOUR NAME and the CAR page's carousel (section 6), from row 6 (2026-11-05) the CHAMPIONSHIP: LEAGUE SELECTION, WELCOME, the races with the LEAGUE REPORT, the points and what winning opens (section 6) and from row 7 (2026-11-05) TIME ATTACK with the ghost car, the FREE RUN and the 3D TRACK page (the turning track map, also in the championship; section 6) and from row 8 (2026-11-05) the OPTIONS ROOM, the team's room with the camera flying between five stations, the menu's own first page (section 6), and from row 10 (2026-11-05) the REPLAY of the race just run (section 6) and from row 11 (2026-11-06) the ARCADE title's four cars (section 6; the page has no scene behind it) and the team's room as the scene of the CAR page of the championship and time attack and of the CAR CUSTOMIZATION page (2026-11-06, section 6 "The garage"). Sources:
OpenScreamer's `docs/NOTES2.md` (S2-5zg, S2-5zk to S2-5zq, S2-5r, S2-5s: the menu read from the decompiled program and
compared with DOSBox), `docs/screamer2/comparison.md` (the menu rows), the game's files (sizes below measured with `tools/dat_sizes.py -v`,

"Checked" below means read from the files or the program in this study; everything else is NOTES2's reading.

## 1. In short

- The original's menu is one loop, `while (page) page();` (`STARTL.EXE` 0x3855b to 0x3867c, checked: the page is an address in
  `0x132494`, every page sets the next and returns, 0 starts a race), over about twenty pages. They fall into four kinds: **2D pages**
  (a 320 x 200 `.BKK` picture, text and sprites), **pages with a few 3D objects** (team badges, letter tyres, track maps, plates,
  cars: the ARCADE title's four cars too) over such a picture, **3D rooms** (the options room, the championship's desk scene: a scene built like a track, drawn by the track's
  engine) and **race overlays** (loading, pause, end of race).
- Nothing in it needs new hardware. The pictures are 8-bit 320 x 200 with their own palette: exactly the framebuffer,
  blitter copies. The 3D objects are meshes with a material library, which the geometry unit draws as the cars' and Screamer 1's menu
  models (`menu.c` did it for 6 maps, 14 cars and 26 letter cubes). The rooms are track images (`MAP1.MMP .UNR .MLB .BCK .COL`: the
  entries `track_build_s2()` already reads, under the name `MENUt1.DAT`), 2.4 to 3 MB of materials, about a hundred and fifty placed pieces at most (the `.MMP` is 10 KB of 64-byte records, a track's 100 to 400 KB): one room fits the
  track window and a frame of it should cost less than a race frame (not measured).
- **No data slots are needed**: since the game's files come from one ZIP (`firmware/zip.c`), the menu's twenty-odd containers are entries of the
  ZIP read on demand (the comparison table's slot count predates the ZIP). `ANIMS/` is not for the arcade page (the earlier study took it for its scene: the page has none, see row 11);
  it is the championship's desk scene's (0x3bc38, built 2026-11-06: section 6), and optional in the ZIP. What does need a change is the **save**: the original's `CHOICE.DAT` is 33,244 bytes (best times, set-ups,
  leagues), the core's slot is 1,120 (`core/data.json` `size_maximum`: a JSON edit, not RTL).
- **Not feasible or not worth it**: the multiplayer pages over a network or a serial link (combat mode, two players on a split screen, is built since 2026-11-13: "Combat mode" below), the CD check
  ("PLEASE INSERT SCREAMER 2 DISK"), the 640 x 400 and 65K-colour variants. The pages' camera moves and fades are cheap.
- Proposed order (section 7): the 2D page toolkit with the loading screen and the pause menu (about 2 days, first), the end-of-race
  screen and game settings, the arcade pages (track pictures, transmission, the 3D car page), the team and name pages, then the
  championship, time attack with its ghost and the best-times table, the options room, the attract sequence, replay and car set-ups.
  Rows 1 to 6 (the complete arcade and the championship, 2D and small 3D) are about 13 days, the whole list about 29 (rough, plus or minus half:
  Screamer 1's code is the template for most rows; comparison.md's 16 to 25 days for the whole game counted the menus as one "large" row).

The kind of game is the mode word `0x130d10` (1 arcade, 2 championship, 4 time attack, 8 free run, 0x40 two players, 0x80 attract, 0x100
replay), set by the room's choice in `0x339e7`; `0x339e7` also builds the race's record (cars, laps, level, set-ups), the page after a
race comes from it. Keys: the pages take only ENTER, ESC and the arrows (`0x38996`); with a controller accelerate is ENTER, brake ESC
(Screamer 1's menu on the Pocket: B or Start = ENTER, A or Select = ESC; the pages follow that).

## 4. The formats (checked on the files)

- **`.BKK`** (the pictures of `GFX/GRAF1.DAT`, 28 of the 320 x 200 `L` set, stored 6 to 41 KB each, 881 KB in all): 24-byte header (`"V652"`, 256 colours,
  width, height, 64,000, 0), the **palette as 768 bytes of 6-bit values (0 to 63)**, then 64,000 raw pixels, 64,792 bytes in all. The palette
  needs `x4` (or `x255/63`) into `VID_PAL`; the pixels are the framebuffer, so a blitter copy (BLIT_COPY | BLIT_OPAQUE) draws it. The `H` set is the
  same at 640 x 400 (256,792 bytes): not used.
  Where the program loads them (flat addresses in `STARTL.EXE`, checked): `load_%c%c` 0x34f19 (the loading screen), `leag_%c%c` 0x3959c and
  0x43fe0, `bkg_4` 0x39989, `bkg_5` 0x3a915, `arcade_%c` 0x3b712, `bkg_6` 0x3bfb9 (a page of the main menu's side, 0x3bf7a) and 0x421aa (the CD message), `tkg_%c%c` 0x3ee6b (the 3D map page 0x3ee28), `leag_0` 0x3f7d6,
  `logo%c` 0x3ffea (TEAM SELECTION), `bkg_2` 0x40c69 (the car and the transmission page, one routine), `bkg_1` 0x41bcd (the track page), `bkg_7` 0x422bb, `hiscore%c` 0x4275f, `bkg_3` 0x433b8.
  Looked at (contact sheet): `LOGOL` and `BKG_1/4/6/7L` are the S2 watermark in other tints, `BKG_2/5L` blue grids, `ARCADE_L` the title,
  `TKG_nL` a track's picture with a column of name plates, `LOAD_nL` a landscape per track, `LEAG_nL` cups and trophies, `HISCOREL` the table frame.
- **`.SPR`, `.FNT`**: `MENUL.SPR` is Screamer 1's format (`firmware/tb_hud.c` header): 111 sprites (count, size, count x x y w h offset, the pixels; 446 KB
  unpacked, 145 KB stored; checked: the sizes add up). `MENUL.FNT` is not: Screamer 2's has a byte with the number of fonts (6, not 7) and per font
  a header of 32 bytes (height, first character, count, a fourth number, the picture's name), then per font count x (width, offset), an `i32` size
  and the pixels, two to a byte, high half first, rows of (width + 1) / 2 bytes (70 KB, 27 KB stored; checked: the sizes add up). Its fonts, by the number the
  original's text routine takes: 0 the titles (29 pixels), 1 and 4 the 18-pixel ones (`font2l`, `font2_2l`), 2 and 3 the 12-pixel (`font3l`, `font3_2l`), 5
  the small digits (7 pixels); characters 32 to 167. Font 1 is glyph for glyph `GAMEL.FNT`'s font 0 (the race screen's, `GFX/GAME1.DAT`).
  A font's colour is a ramp of 15 palette entries (colour * 16 + shade, shades 1 to 15): colour 0 grey and orange, 1 white, 2 gold, in every
  `.BKK` palette and every track's `MAPn.COL` alike. The sprites carry the logos, arrows, bars, the track banners (0x13 to 0x19, 144 x 50) and the
  cars' side views. The blitter draws sprites (BLIT_COPY, 0 see-through) and fonts (BLIT_FONT, shades).
- **Room, scene, map, badge, plate, letter**: the containers' entries are `.MLB` (materials and textures, as a track's), `.UNR` + `.MMP` + `.BCK`
  + `.COL` (a track's, for rooms and scenes), `.3DX` (a mesh with blocks, as the cars and props), `.MCM` (camera stations), `.PTH` (object paths,
  the `SPLINES.PTH` of a scene and `DATA/GEN`'s `SCENE0..3.PTH`). The firmware reads all of them already (`tb_*.c`, `s2car.c`, `track.c`) but the `.PTH` paths and the `.MCM` camera records (the race's trackside cameras
  are the same records, `CAMERASn.MCM`).
- **Text**: `DATA/LOCAL.DAT`'s `STRINGS.TXT` (2.6 KB, `*` lines are comments, "max chars n" tell the widths), `GAME.TXT`, `ATTRACT.TXT`; read by `s2menu_init()`
  for the start menu.

## 5. The core's path, screen by screen

- **Blitter + console (2D)**: copy a picture (0.9 ms: 64,000 pixels at one a clock), sprites, `BLIT_FONT` text, fills, lines. Palette fades are a per-frame palette
  write (`pal_faded()`), the darkened still is the radar's table (`efx_darken`). Everything Screamer 1's menu pages, loading screen, pause and end of race do.
- **Geometry unit (3D-o)**: a piece = a mesh + a matrix; a race frame hands it 200 or more pieces, the maps, plates, badges and cars of a menu page
  are 1 to 30. The menu's models use their own material file (`GU_MLB`), as Screamer 1's `menu.c` set it. A ring of 26 letter tyres is far below a race frame's pieces. The heaviest page is the track map of time attack and league: measured on the PC (row 7, section 6) it hands the unit 1 to 7 pieces and draws 342 to 883 faces a frame (California 794,
  Switzerland's 871 the most), less than a 4-car race frame (1,146 faces handed to the unit on average along the seven roads, `tools/s2/framestats.sh sweep`), so no level of detail was needed (not timed on the Pocket). Cars come from `s2teams.c` (the grid's four, or all five of a team, 1 MB).
- **Track engine (3D-r)**: `track_build_s2()` with the room's entries (`MAP1.*` of `MENUt1.DAT` instead of `GFX/MAPnH01.DAT` + `DATA/MAPn.DAT`: no road, no walls, no
  line, no scenery) and a camera from `P_t.MCM`; no physics. A scene's objects need the object paths (`SPLINES.PTH`; the arcade title's cars have `GEN.DAT`'s `SCENEn.PTH` and no scene) and the cars' placement along
  them (`road.c` places cars by a road point; paths give position and heading per time: `s2anim_paths_*`). The frame cost is the number of faces in view: a room (150 pieces at most) is
  below a track's (400 to 1,100 faces). Built and counted for the options room (row 8, section 6): 11 to 24 pieces and 244 to 958 faces a frame at the five stations, 34 pieces and 1,196 faces at the first frame of the fly-in at most.
- **Memory**: a room or scene image is built in the track window (`0x1210_0000`, 7 MB; nothing else lives there between races), the 2D pictures (64 KB
  each) and `MENUL` (0.5 MB) in the SDRAM below the cars' blobs, the way `menu.c` placed them in Screamer 1 (`MENU_MEM`, 0.5 MB). Reading: a stored ZIP is read by
  range (`zip_read_range`: the container's directory and the entries a page needs, like `s2_cars_load()`), a deflated one whole (`GRAF1.DAT` 4.2 MB; `SCENEnn1.DAT`, 4.2 MB, the desk scene's, is stored in the ZIP as the game's folder has it: by range). At the Pocket's 1.2 MB/s and 3 MB/s for the LZW: a 2D page 35 KB read and 65 KB unpacked, about 50 ms; a room 0.8 MB read, 2.9 MB unpack, about 1.7 s; a scene's one `MAPn`
  (about 2 MB) and the team's cars about 2 s (counted by bytes in section 6: the desk scene reads 1.1 to 1.25 MB and unpacks 2.0 to 2.4 MB). Pages that must change at once (the 5 stations of the room) stay loaded.
- **Slots**: none needed for the menu (ZIP). Free: 24 of 32 (firmware, log, program, ZIP, music 2, CHOICE.DAT, ATTACK.DAT are 8).
- **Saves** (done, section 7.1): the original's `CHOICE.DAT` (33,244 bytes: name, volumes, view, settings, leagues and unlocks, set-ups 0x188 per team, best times: lists of 15 rows of time,
  name, team, model, replay number, per kind, track and length of race) did not fit the 1,120 bytes of the Screamer 1 layout the core's slot had (and the start menu borrowed
  fields of). `firmware/s2save.c` keeps a Screamer 2 layout of our own in the same slot, 19,348 bytes, with `core/data.json`'s `size_maximum` raised to 36,864
  (the Pocket writes back what the firmware keeps).
  The ghost (the original records 0x20 bytes a frame, 7,000 frames, per track) is re-simulated from the controls, which the physics' fixed 20 ms step allows: **one byte a step** (the Pocket's buttons are on or off: gas, brake,
  left, right, handbrake, gear up, gear down, the gearbox; since 2026-11-14 two bytes a step for a run driven with a stick, docs/screamer2/driving.md "The controls"), three laps 8,000 to 11,000 steps, 7 tracks of 51,440 bytes each in `ATTACK.DAT`'s 360,096 bytes (built, row 7: **the slot was not changed**, see section 6).

### 7.1 The save (row 2)

`firmware/s2save.c` / `s2save.h`, test `tools/s2/check_save_s2.sh`. **Where and when it is written**: the save is the nonvolatile data slot 23
(`CHOICE.DAT`, `core/data.json` `size_maximum` 36,864) and lives in the SDRAM at `0x13F0_0000`, 64 KB of window up to `ATTACK.DAT`. The firmware never writes
the file: the Pocket reads the slot out of the SDRAM through the bridge when the core is quit or switched off (docs/soc.md, "Saved games"). So there is no dirty flag and no
"write back": every call that changes the save seals the changed part (its CRC-32) at once, and what the SDRAM holds is always what the next start reads. At boot `s2save_load()`
(called by `save_load()`, early in `prog_main`) reads the slot and puts its size, 19,348 bytes, into the APF's slot table (`slot_set_size`), because the APF keeps a size only for a file it
found and writes the slot back with it: an existing shorter file (the 1,120 bytes of the old layout) is read by its own size and is replaced by the full-size one at the next quit.
`size_maximum` is only the limit for what the Pocket may load: 36,864 leaves room for later versions of the layout; more than that (the window ends at 64 KB) needs `data.json` raised again, and
`data.json` is read when the core is loaded, so a change of it needs a full deploy.

**The layout** (little-endian, all offsets in bytes; the numbers in brackets are the defaults, which are the original's first start where it has one):

| Offset | Size | Field |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 4 | marker `S2SV` |
| 4 | 2 | version (1) |
| 6 | 2 | size of the layout (19,348) |
| 8 | 4 | CRC-32 of the main part (bytes 16 .. 447) |
| 12 | 4 | CRC-32 of the best times (bytes 448 .. 19,347) |
| 16 | 4 | the player's name: three printable characters and 0 (`MIL`) |
| 20 | 8 | the GAME SETTINGS rows, each the index of its value in the page's list: TRANSMISSION (0 AUTOMATIC), DETAIL LEVEL (2 HIGH), NUMBER OF LAPS (1: 3 laps), ARCADE ENEMIES SKILL (1 NORMAL), MULTIPLAYER ENEMIES (0 OFF), TIME ATTACK GHOST CAR (0 ON), DAMAGES (0 OFF), MUSIC (0 ON). The start menu's laps, level and gearbox rows are the rows 2, 3 and 0 |
| 28 | 6 | volumes: effects, CD, FM music; three `u16`, 0 .. 0x7FFF, 0x6000 is the nominal level of `snd_master()`, `music_master()`, `fm_master()` (0x6000 each) |
| 34 | 1 | the view a race starts in (the game's view number 1 .. 7; 2, the first from the car) |
| 35 | 3 | the start menu's last choices: track 1 .. 7, team 1 .. 4, car 1 .. 5 as the files number them (1, 1, 1) |
| 38 | 1 | the highest league open, 0 .. 3 (0) |
| 39 | 1 | tracks opened beyond the first three, 0 .. 4 (0) |
| 40 | 8 | reserved, 0; a later field takes one of these bytes if its 0 is a valid value (or clamps to one): no new version |
| 48 | 400 | per team (4 x 100): the fifth (bonus) car won (1 byte, 0), the set-ups (4 cars x Custom 1 .. 4 x 6 numbers, `int8` -1 .. 4, each set `2 2 0 2 2 2`: HANDLING, TIRE PRESSURE, TIRE TYPE, FRONT SUSPENSIONS, REAR SUSPENSIONS, BRAKES), 3 bytes unused |
| 448 | 18,900 | the best times: 105 lists of 15 rows of 12 bytes |

A **row**: `float time` in seconds (+0; 3599.999 for a row nobody has driven, as the game's file has it), `char name[4]` (+4, three characters and 0), `u8 team` 1 .. 4 (+8),
`u8 car` 1 .. 5 (+9), `u16 replay` (+10; 0 for none). An empty row is `3599.999`, `MIL`, team (row & 3) + 1, car 1, 0, as the game starts them. A **list** is the quickest 15 times of
one kind of game, track and thing timed; list number `(kind * 7 + track - 1) * 5 + list`: kind 0 league (CHAMPIONSHIP), 1 arcade, 2 time attack (the game's numbering), track 1 .. 7,
list 0 .. 3 the total time of a race of 1, 3, 5 or 10 laps (the NUMBER OF LAPS values), list 4 the best single lap. Time attack has only the 3-lap totals: a total list asked for with
kind 2 is list 1.

**Loading** (`s2save_load()`; one log line, `save: CHOICE.DAT <how> (<bytes> bytes)`): at most 19,348 bytes of the file are read, whatever its size is (a longer file is read to the layout's end;
a size that is not a multiple of 4 loses its last bytes). Then: no file or under 4 bytes, a failed read, a file that is not ours (marker missing and not 1,120 bytes), or another version or
size field give the defaults for everything. A file of the right marker and version is checked **part by part**: the main part must be whole, match its CRC-32 and hold only values in range (and a
printable name); the times must be whole, match theirs and hold only times 0 .. 3599.999, printable names and teams and cars in range. A part that fails is replaced by its defaults and the other is
kept (a short file keeps the main part if it reaches byte 448). A file of exactly 1,120 bytes without the marker is the **old layout**, Screamer 1's block whose unused fields held the start menu's choices
(`track`, `intro` = team, `car`, `laps`, `difficulty`, and `failed` = `0x5332xxxx | gearbox`): with the marker `0x5332` in the high half of `failed` the six choices are taken over (clamped), everything else is the
defaults; without it the defaults. The next quit writes the new layout, so this happens once. Every getter answers within its range, before `s2save_load()` too (whatever the SDRAM holds is clamped).

**The original's file, for the steps to come** (decoded from `CONFIG/CHOICE.DAT` of the game: 0x2077 words, word *i* stored as `(v + i * 0x1050c) ^ i`; OpenScreamer's `docs/NOTES2.md` S2-5zk to S2-5zn and

The BEST TIME / BEST LAP of the LOADING screen are the first rows of the total list of the chosen length and of the lap list (time attack: the 3-lap list).
The routine that puts a finished race into the lists, `STARTL.EXE` 0x353cb (`tools/dis.sh -f 0x353cb 0x358c9`, read for this step): for every car that finished (time attack: only the player's) the total time goes into

place is the first row the new time is not slower than (`fcomp`, `ja`), the rows below move down one, the new row gets replay 0, the car's team, model and name; then the original names a replay for it
(not in time attack): the smallest number from 1 not used in that list's replay numbers, `REP<length><level><track><letter>`.

**What differs from the original**: our lists are rows of 12 bytes (not columns of words), teams and cars are counted from 1, the file has a CRC-32 per part in place of the original's word-by-word scrambling, the volumes use
the firmware's scale (0x6000 nominal) so that nothing sounds different, and the HI SCORES page's choices, the attract counter and the league in progress are not kept. The original's file is not read (no conversion of
the game's files; a `CHOICE.DAT` of the game put into the slot is a foreign file and gives the defaults). **Not checked on the Pocket**: the write-back of the longer file (the first quit after the new firmware turns the owner's 1,120-byte file
into 19,348 bytes), and the migration with the owner's real file.

### The sounds of the end screen and the pages, the arcade grid (2026-11-06)

**What the original fades, and when** (read from `STARTL.EXE`, `tools/dis.sh -f`; measured where it says so). The only fade of a sound in the program is the sound manager's **master volume**
(its object at `[0x124274]`, the field `+0x18`, which HMI's digital driver applies over every voice): `0xaee60(m, target, step)` moves it by `step` towards `target` (up: add and clamp; down:
subtract and clamp, also when it would pass the target or the 0x7fff) and is called once a frame by seven places, always with the target 0 and `step = (int)(dt x constant)` with the frame's
time `dt` (`[0x133350]`): the pause menu (`0x4a15a`, 32767 a second, and back at once with `step = volume` at its end), the three one-player end screens (the arcade's `0x47636`,
the championship's `0x48041`, time attack's `0x48b4c`: **16383 a second, from the first frame after the screen's clock `[0x133050]` has passed 4.0 s**, the moment the choices come up; the
first four seconds the sound goes on whole), the two-player end screen (`0x472f4`, same rate, at once) and the championship's desk scene (`0x3bc38`: 28671 a second as the page ends for the
race: the menu sounds die away; that scene is not built here). The master starts at the settings' effects volume, 0x5fff by default (`0x32de6`).
**Nothing else fades.** The CD is `0x4d784` (play a track, repeat flag), `0x4d908` (stop) and `0x4d990` (volume, set whole, never eased): the race's set-up stops and plays the song with the volume
at 0 and then at the setting (`0x2eb9d` .. `0x2ec00`), the pause's CD PLAYER sets volumes, the menu pages play track 2 and stop it (`0x38951`, `0x3bd29`, `0x3cc1e`, `0x42462`; `0x38991`,
`0x3bc46`, `0x3cc0f`, `0x42451`), the race's end calls `0x31495` which stops it; no call is made by the race loop (it polls the drive, `0x4d800`, every 0.4 s to play the song again), by the
finish (`0x201ee`) or by any end screen. A page change is a palette fade and no more (pages are cut in, fade out over 13 to 25 frames); the pages' sounds are one click each (`MENU.DTR`), none of them
eased.

**Measured in the running original** (`python -I tools/s2/dosbox_polish.py 2 --fade`, an arcade race on Columbia, one lap, the player's car handed to the game's driver, the object read at
every change from DOSBox's memory, 670 samples): the master is 24575 (0x5fff) from the finish and stays so until the end screen's clock passes 4.0 s; the first drop is seen at clock 4.004,
every frame takes `(int)(dt x 16383)` off it (360 at the DOSBox's dt of 0.0220 s, 68 frames), the last takes the 95 that are left (the clamp at 0), 69 frames and 1.50 s of the game's clock
from the first step to 0, which is 24575 / 16383. The tool's `--check` reads the files again and says whether the numbers are as the code has them.

**What the core does.** `s2end_frame()` (s2end.c) asks `s2sound_effects()` for the level of the race's effects from the first frame the clock has passed 4.0 s: 0x8000 less 437 a video frame (16383
a second of the master 0x5fff), 75 frames to nothing; the first four seconds ask nothing. `s2sound.c` keeps the level (`fx_level`) and writes every voice's register at it, so the engines, tyres
and scenery of the race that goes on under the screen, and the knocks that start, stay at it; `s2pause.c` now eases at the pause's own 32767 a second (874 a frame, 0.75 s: it was a second). The
**music is no longer faded out at the finish**: that was Screamer 1's rule; the song plays on through the end screen (as the original's CD does), RACE AGAIN and REPLAY find it playing and keep it
(the pause's RESTART always did), and EXIT stops it with the race. The Pocket's log of the owner's first race had `ph1.5/200.6` for the visit's player's step: the worst
step was **the RACE AGAIN after the finish** (the time step of the owner's log is not in the repository: this is the reading of the code, not a measurement), which started the song again (faded
out and stopped three seconds after the finish) with the ring's first fill, 224 KB and about 170 ms, inside the frame's step, and the race's own set-up on top; the song is not stopped any more, so
there is nothing to fill. (Not found: any music that starts *at* the finish.) The first fill in pieces over several frames was looked at then and left out (the mixer would have played what the ring held before); it is built since 2026-11-07 with the mixer off until the ring has 96 KB (below, "The menu's music").
### The menu's music (2026-11-07)

**Where the original plays CD track 2, and where not** (`STARTL.EXE`, `tools/dis.sh -f`; the CD calls are `0x4d784` play(track, repeat), `0x4d908` stop, `0x4d990` volume, `0x4d8c0` pause, `0x4d928` let go, `0x4d800` the poll; the
MUSIC setting is `[0x128a44]`, 0 for ON, and the race record's `0x130d64` is that, `0x33a35`; every start also needs a CD, `[0x4dad0]` 0; track 2 is the first audio track of `GAME.DAT`'s cue sheet, the races' songs are tracks 3 to 9
by `CD_TRACKS`). The song is `music_cd(2)` with the repeat flag, in these places and no others (the 9 call sites of play and the 15 of stop in the program were all looked at; the others are the pause menu's CD PLAYER, `0x49e54` .. `0x4a024`, the drive's own poll and start-up, `0x4d86d`, `0x4d13e`):

| What | Where | Call |
|---|---|---|
| plays track 2 (stop first, with MUSIC ON) | the end of the attract sequence's routine (`0x3ca20`, any way it ends; the next page is TEAM SELECTION) | `0x3cc0f` stop, `0x3cc1e` play (volume `0x3cc01`) |
| plays track 2 | the program's menu loop each time it begins again, which is after every race of every kind (`0x38557`: the race is run from this loop when the page chain ends, the loop begins again at `0x385e4`) | `0x38942` stop, `0x38951` play (volume `0x38934`) |
| restarts track 2 | the championship's desk scene (`0x3bc38`): stopped at its start, played from the beginning once the scene is loaded | `0x3bc46` stop, `0x3bd29` play |
| starts / stops it | GAME SETTINGS' MUSIC row (`0x42380` ..): after the value changed, OFF stops, ON plays from the beginning | `0x42451` stop, `0x42462` play |
| stops it | the attract sequence's start (`0x3ca3a`; its race is set up with the track's song, paused at once and let go at the third stage, `0x2ebea`, `0x4d928`) | `0x3ca3a` |
| stops it | the program loop when a race is to be run (`0x386bd`), the race's own set-up (`0x2e200`, `0x2ebc4`, which plays the track's song with the volume at 0 and then at the setting) | `0x386bd`, `0x2e200`, `0x2ebc4` |
| stops it | the race's end (`0x3156c`, with the volumes put back) and the program's end (`0x38991`) | `0x3156c`, `0x38991` |

So: the options room, TEAM SELECTION, the arcade pages, the championship's pages and CAR pages (and CUSTOMIZE), time attack's pages and GAME SETTINGS all have track 2 playing, because it was begun by the attract sequence's end or by the end of the
last race; **no page starts, changes, fades or stops it** (the page loop's frame, `0x38dbb`, only calls the poll `0x4d800` when MUSIC is ON, which starts the song again when the drive reports it has ended: the song loops). Nothing fades
the CD (the only fade of a sound is the master volume, above); the end screens, the pause menu and RACE AGAIN leave it as it is. MUSIC OFF: none of the starts happens, and the poll is not called. The CD is a separate device: the song plays through
every load and page change without a gap or a restart (the loads of the original are from the drive or the disk while the drive plays by itself).

**What the core does.** `firmware/s2menumusic.c`, `music.c`, `slot.c`, `s2page.c`; docs/development.md "The menus' music" has the design and the tests.
- *Where it begins:* not at the events of the table but when a page begins outside a race and no song plays or waits (`s2mm_page()`, called by the page runner `s2page_run_step()`): a page follows each start of the table (the attract sequence's end, the end of a race), and the song then begins where the
  page is up, not in front of a load. `viewer.c` `load_track()` stops it (the race begins: `0x386bd`), the race's songs start as they did (`s2snd_race_start`), the race's end stops the race's song (`s2snd_stop`, as before). The desk scene calls `s2mm_song()` once its scene is loaded
  (the restart, `0x3bd29`), GAME SETTINGS' MUSIC row calls it when the value changed (ON: from the beginning, OFF: silence; `0x42462`, `0x42451`). The pause menu and the end screen are pages of a race: they start nothing (`s2snd_racing()`).
- *No gap is not possible, a short one is:* the ring is the CPU's work and the data slot is shared with every blocking read. `slot.c` asks `music.c` before each blocking call (`slot_hook`): a read of the ring that runs is waited for (the slot does one at a time), and a song
  that plays is **held** (stopped, its place kept to the sector) when the call would not leave the ring enough (the read's estimated time at 0.74 MB/s, the CPU's work on its bytes as long again, and 0.3 s; or 0.5 s without a `music_frame()` in a chain of reads such as a ZIP entry's chunks).
  The next `music_frame()` (every page frame) resumes it: it begins to fill the ring again and plays from the place it was held. So a **long load is a gap of its length and about a tenth of a second** (the options room's 1.5 s build when the room is not in memory, the desk scene's 1.0 to 1.2 MB, the cars' 0.5 s load if
  it comes with the ring low; **the song goes on where it stopped, it does not restart**), where the original has none; **short reads do not chop it**: a page's picture or a sprite sheet (up to 64 KB, 90 ms) always passes, a read of up to about 380 KB on a settled ring.
- *The first fill:* the 170 ms of the first fill of the ring (the 200 ms step on the owner's Pocket at a race restart) is gone for every start of a song, the race's, the attract sequence's and the menu's: `cd_start()` only starts the first read; `music_frame()` brings the reads in one a frame and starts the mixer when 96 KB are there
  (3 reads, 0.55 s of the song; the song is audible 9 frames, 0.18 s, after its start at the model's 1 MB/s; the real mixer in the simulation played it without a frame of the old ring, development.md "The menus' music"), so no frame waits and the mixer never plays what the ring held before. (The first reading of this, in the polish agent's notes above, saw the risk of an early start; the mixer is simply off until the ring has enough.)
  The loading screen's fade calls `music_frame()` (`s2load_fade()`), so the race's song is heard from the fade on as before.

**The arcade grid.** `s2menu_grid()` gives slot k the team `(team + k) mod 4` and the car `(car + k) mod 4` (0-based; the bonus car, 4, gives the computers 1 2 3), as `0x33b0e` .. `0x33bc9` does
in every kind of game (the championship's `s2l_grid()` had it; the last league's computers drive the bonus car, only there). Measured in the original: a private CHOICE.DAT with team 2 (WASP) and its
second car, an arcade race on tracks 2 and 5: the entries at `0x130c58 + 0x2c i` hold teams 2 3 0 1 and cars 1 2 3 0 (`dosbox_polish.py`, `bytes_grid.txt`), which is the rule. The old rule
(the other teams in order, each with the car of its own number) differed for most computers' cars of the 20 choices but those of the default; the test (`tools/host/menu_screen.c`) holds
the new rule against a second writing of the code's loop and the old one as the negative control.

### Combat mode: two players on a split screen (2026-11-13)

The MULTIPLAYER MENU's first item. The original (`STARTL.EXE` 0x39901, 0x3a8e4, the race's set-up 0x34d4a, the mode word's bit 0x40), read from the program and from the running game
(`tools/s2/dosbox_arcade.py --combat <steps>`: the two players' keys are those of `CONFIG/CONTROLS.CFG`, the arrows with right Shift and Ctrl, and W X A D with 2 and 1):

- **The pages.** Both players' pages are on the screen at once, each on his half (320 x 100): TEAM SELECTION, then ENTER YOUR NAME (accelerate goes on, brake goes back, left / right
  choose, the gear keys move the name's cursor), `WAITING PLAYER 2` on the half of the one who is done; then CAR SELECTION and TRANSMISSION the same way (0x3a8e4); then the arcade's
  TRACK page on the whole screen, whose ENTER starts the race. No page asks for the laps: they are the settings' (0x130d48 from the table at 0x1131fc).
- **The race.** Two cars and no computers (0x130c48 = 2), on the grid's first row with the second player in front (car i of n takes place n - 1 - i). The first player's view is the
  screen's upper half, the second's the lower: the same routine draws both, the second 100 rows down (0x2b880 adds 32,000 to the buffer's address), each with the camera of its car
  (camera flags 0x29 instead of 0x9: bit 0x20 is the half view, 0x12ffb: its rows are 200 / 2 - 2 and its eye line 0.65 of them, as the whole view's), then a line at row 99. Both
  cameras start in the view the player left his last race in, INSIDE's dashboard becoming INCAR (0x2e9bc), and no key selects INSIDE in a half (0x1335d).
- **The race screen** of each half is the single one's at its places from the half's top, the dial 100 rows up, without its words and clocks: the lap and the laps, the place
  (the laurel for the leader), the bar, the bend's arrow, the dial. The start lights are drawn once, over both halves.
- **The end.** A player who has done his laps gets `1ST POSITION` / `2ND POSITION` and his time on his half (white rows 45 to 55 and 62 to 74 of the half) while the other still
  races; when both have, RACE AGAIN and EXIT in halves at the bottom of the screen, as the arcade's end without a replay. No times are kept.

Here (`firmware/viewer.c` `combat_run()`, `frame_split()`; `race.h` `race_combat`; `s2hud.h` `s2hud_split()`; `s2end.h` `s2end_split()`):

- **The second player's controller** is the Dock's second: the core's register `IN_KEY2` (`cont2_key`, docs/soc.md; the bitstream of 2026-11-13). Its d-pad steers: the core has no
  register for a second controller's stick. Without a second controller the menu's item answers SECOND CONTROLLER NEEDED. `in_pad` (hw.h) says which controller `in_keys()` reads:
  each player's pages read his own, the TRACK page, the pause menu and the end's choices either.
- **The pages** are the arcade's own, whole-screen, one player after the other and not both at once on halves: TEAM SELECTION for player 1, for player 2, the CAR page with its
  TRANSMISSION state for player 1, for player 2 (PLAYER 1 / PLAYER 2 under the title), then the TRACK page; ESC goes back a page. There are no names (the race shows none). The first
  player's choices are the save's, as the arcade's; the second's are kept while the core runs (he begins with the next team's first car). **A stand-in**: the original's half pages
  with both players choosing at once are not built.
- **The race** is the arcade's with `race_combat`: two cars with a player's controls each (`race_step_player()`, `race_step_player2()`), the cars touching as the arcade's, no
  recording and no replay. The buttons are each player's own controller's, as in every race (B gas, Y brake, A handbrake, L / R gears, X his next view: INCAR, NEAR, BACK). Start on
  either is the pause menu. No mirror.
- **The sound** is the original's of this mode: each car's engine is heard from its own player's camera (0x1fe18: the cars after the first take the second camera as the
  listener), so both engines are near and neither player hears the other's car; both cars' tyres are heard, each on a voice of its own (0x1b77e: the mode's every car, where a
  single race has the followed car's alone); the scenery is heard from the first camera. The knocks at a wall and an obstacle and the landing are the first car's alone, as the
  original's are the followed car's (0x11f0c8, which is car 0 in the running game's combat race); a knock of the two cars is both players'.
- **The picture**: two passes of the scene a frame, each 99 rows from the half's top (rows 99 and 199 black; the eye line is 0.65 of the rows, row 64), each with its own camera,
  backdrop and list of objects; the second view's objects are made when the rasterizer has drawn the first (the shadows' and the puffs' blocks are one set). A frame takes about twice
  the single view's CPU work for the scene: the race runs at 25 pictures a second where the single one runs at 50.
- **Not built**: the trackside cameras after the finish (the view stays the player's), the checkpoint gap and the 1ST mark over the leading car (the original's halves were not seen
  to show them), names, the half pages.
